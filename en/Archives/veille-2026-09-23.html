The future is negotiating its terms of access.

Algorithmic fairness out for consultation – Amazon shuts the door on agents – natural-disaster status restarts the clock – sovereignty is counted in megawatts. Hello, visitors from the future: this week nobody is promising a new world; everyone is writing the terms and conditions of the one on its way. Older context is dated. Here we go.

In 30 seconds

  • Insurance: the ACPR consultation on algorithmic fairness closes on 30 September.
  • Technology: Amazon blocks Meta's agent, which already knows how to switch a car insurance policy.
  • Risk: a natural-disaster order published in the Official Journal opens a 30-day window to file.
  • Work: one manager in two uses AI every week; training has not followed everywhere.
1Insurance & Insurtech

Pricing risk without sorting people.

There is one week left to answer the regulator. on 1 July the ACPR published a discussion paper on algorithmic fairness in the financial sector, open until 30 September 2026 to insurers, banks, fintechs, insurtechs, consumer associations and researchers. The most unsettling point in the text is not a new obligation: it is the finding that “fairness by ignorance” — removing sensitive data from the model — no longer protects anyone, since an algorithm reconstructs the information through proxy variables. The regulator states that this document does not express its official position : this is a step in method, not a doctrine. It lands just as the sector's money runs in a single direction: Gallagher Re counts $1.63 billion invested in insurtech in the first quarter of 2026of which: 95% of it into AI-centred companies. Funding automated pricing quickly while debating its biases slowly: the timing has its irony.

A model can be fair on paper and discriminatory in its outputs: it is the output that must be measured, not the list of variables removed at the input. Seven days remain to tell the regulator what the field is seeing.

2Tech & AI

The agent wanted to go shopping. The door was shut.

The debate on autonomous agents has just moved from the lab to the doorstep. Amazon is blocking Muse, Meta's personal agent, from its retail site : since 20 September, an agent that connects gets a message about breaching the conditions of use. The official argument is security — an unidentified third party moving through a customer account and its order history, with credentials Amazon suspects the agent of storing, what Meta denied from launch day, 8 September, stating that Muse has “no visibility” into passwords or payment methods. The implicit argument is less noble: advertising is worth more than $68 billion in annual revenue, and an agent that buys without looking at the pages sees no ads. The precedent is known: Amazon had already served notice on Perplexity over its Comet agent, with an injunction won and then overturned on appeal on 4 August, the court holding that the user, not the AI company, was the one accessing the site. One detail should catch the sector's attention: among the first reported uses of Muse, now the number one free app in the US App Store a week after launch is switching a car insurance policy. Nobody has asked the insurer what it thinks.

An agent that cancels and subscribes on the customer's behalf changes the nature of the distribution journey. Three questions to ask now: do our terms of use say anything about third-party agents, can we recognise them, and do we want to welcome them or block them?

3Economy & Finance

Inflation takes out a subscription.

Projections make less noise than a rate decision, and sometimes carry more weight. in September, ECB staff published their macroeconomic projections for the euro areain a context where monetary policy decisions are still published meeting by meeting. Yet the average hides a considerable gap: in August 2026 inflation ranged from 2.7% in France and 2.9% in Germany up to 5.8% in Lithuania and 5.2% in Cyprus. A single policy for realities three points apart: borrowers, savers and insurers are not living through the same year depending on the country. Meanwhile, private capital keeps flowing towards a single keyword: €42.2 million raised by nine French start-ups from 14 to 18 September, 82% of it by six AI-built companies, modest cheques compared with the first half. These are weekly flows, not a demonstrated trend.

A single policy rate produces very unequal effects depending on the local market: thinking “euro area” about a savings or lending portfolio means averaging situations that have not converged.

4Regulation & Risk

The Official Journal restarts the clock.

An administrative text can trigger several thousand procedures overnight. an interministerial order of 11 September recognising a state of natural disaster was published in the Official Journal on 22 September, covering floods, mudslides and ground movements linked to clay shrink-swell. Locally, the effect is immediate: sixteen municipalities in the Nord are recognised, while in Le Port-Marly the order still covers only a landslide from the spring — recognition is decided municipality by municipality, claim by claim. Two figures are worth remembering before the questions start: 30 days to file a claim from the publication of the order and a deductible raised to €1,520 when the damage comes from drought and soil rehydration, against €380 for an ordinary natural disaster. A recognised resident can therefore be compensated and still be left with a sum to cover.

The 30 days run from publication, not from the discovery of the damage, and the drought deductible is four times the ordinary one. Two facts that change a customer conversation.

5People & Work

One manager in two has the habit. Not the training.

Adoption came before learning, and the gap is starting to show. According to Apec's study “Les cadres et l'IA”, one manager in two now uses an AI tool at least once a week, 15 points more than a year ago, peaking at 62% among younger managers. The shift is reaching job applications too: 31% of managers have used generative AI to apply for a job, in a looser market where only half of recruiters report difficulties. Volumes, meanwhile, are holding up: 305,800 managerial hires expected in 2026, up 4%, with IT accounting for a fifth of them. But daily use is not mastery: the CNIL survey of data protection officers found that 85% of them had received no AI-specific training — a report published in July 2026, based on answers from November 2025. Everyone is using it; few yet know when not to.

A usage rate measures a habit, not a skill. The real question to ask a team: can we spot an answer that is wrong but plausible, and take back control at the right moment?

6Sovereignty & Infrastructure

Sovereignty is counted in megawatts.

Speeches about digital independence always end at an electrical connection. Orange and the investor Morrison have announced plans for a data centre joint venture in France : 50/50 ownership, a 400 MW target, close to ten times current capacity, backed by five data centres contributed across four campuses — Chevilly-Larue, Aubervilliers, Chartres, Val-de-Reuil — and a €3 billion investment programme. Two caveats: this is an exclusivity agreement, with the joint venture only targeted for the first quarter of 2027, and announced capacity is not delivered capacity. The European framework points the same way: in June 2026 the Commission presented its Cloud and AI Development Act to close the compute gap and reduce dependence on non-European hyperscalers. Hosting in Europe will not be enough: sovereignty does not live in a building, it also plays out in contract clauses and in the law that applies.

Hosting on European soil says nothing about the law that applies to the data or about how reversible the contract is. Megawatts are a condition, never the guarantee.
