The future cuts its prices. Not its demands.

Cheaper models – insurance breaks down barriers – training gets practical – 2G bows out. Hello, visitors to the future: announcements are multiplying, but real progress is what we can use, explain and verify. Older context is dated. Here we go.

In 30 seconds

  • Insurance: marketing, data and AI come closer together in governance.
  • Technology: suppliers announce cheaper models; real-world costs still need testing.
  • Work: Impulsion IA 2027 promises training and support, with results still to be delivered.
  • Infrastructure: the end of 2G puts safety equipment back in the spotlight.
1Insurance & Insurtech

AI moves beyond the IT department.

An appointment can reveal more than a demo. On 22 September, Crédit Agricole Assurances appointed Philippe Toulorge Director of Digital and Customer Marketing. The remit brings together marketing, data, artificial intelligence and technology platforms : this is an organisational shift, not just a change in vocabulary. The announcement sets out goals to improve customer understanding and simplify customer journeys. Those are stated ambitions, not measured improvements in satisfaction. For context, the insurer's strategy links transformation, efficiency and delivery on its customer promise. The useful signal for insurance is bringing technology closer to the actual customer journey, where protection needs, understanding the policy and service quality meet. An organisation chart cannot fix everything; it does show where a company wants to remove barriers.

The test lies in the customer journey: less repeated data entry, clearer explanations and help at the right moment. A new department needs to deliver more than a new email signature.

2Tech & AI

Models move up. Prices come down.

The intelligence race is also moving to the invoice. OpenAI is introducing GPT-6 Sol and Luna, while Anthropic launched Opus 5.5 on 22 September. OpenAI lists Sol at $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens, versus promotional GPT-5.6 Sol prices of $4 and $20. Anthropic, meanwhile, lists $4 and $20 for Opus 5.5, and estimates a 40% reduction in cost on typical workloads compared with Opus 5. Price per token and cost per task are different measures. These are supplier announcements, not our own benchmark results. For summarising a file or extracting information, a useful comparison also includes retries, errors and human review. A discounted model can be expensive if the work has to be done again.

Comparing cost per accepted result on the same files beats comparing two price tags. Lower prices open up uses; they do not remove the need to check answers.

3Economy & Finance

Europe has savings. It needs a smoother route to investment.

Capital is not always scarce; the route to a project can be unnecessarily difficult. In an article published by the Banque de France on 22 September, Agnès Bénassy-Quéré puts EU household saving at €1,642 billion in 2025. That is an annual flow, not a pot of cash available in full: €995 billion finances housing and €647 billion constitutes financial saving. In the second instalment, also dated 22 September, she argues for reducing financing costs and fragmentation. The framework is the Savings and Investments Union, a strategy presented by the Commission on 19 March 2025. This is an economic policy direction, not a promised return. For insurance, our take concerns intermediation: explaining fees, risk and how long money is tied up matters as much as promoting the innovation being financed. The future needs capital, not opaque brochures.

Getting savings moving requires trust and understandable choices. An investment presented as strategic still needs to explain its risk and time horizon.

4Regulation & Risk

DPOs need an AI instruction manual.

Adding a responsibility does not create the hours needed to fulfil it. On 22 September, CNIL published the English version of its assessment of DPOs and AI. The timeline matters: the first French publication dates from 3 July 2026 and the survey draws on responses from 2,390 DPOs collected from 5 to 21 November 2025. It is therefore not a September snapshot. The gap is still revealing: 55% of respondents say they already handle AI Act responsibilities, but 85% have not received specific AI training. These statements describe the respondents, not every French business. Our take: before assigning governance to an existing role, define its scope, resources and supporting functions. Adding “AI” to a job description does not download expertise.

Responsibility without training or dedicated time becomes a neatly labelled blind spot. The person's title matters less than their actual ability to act.

5People & Work

AI training: the plan is out, the work begins.

There is still no “train everyone” button. On 22 September, the government presented Impulsion IA 2027: 12 measures and €80 million in new funding, according to AFP. The Ministry of Labour sets out four priorities: training, business support, social dialogue and anticipating effects on work. This builds on Osez l'IA, launched on 1 July 2025. The issue goes beyond learning to ask an assistant a question: the minister also acknowledges risks of more intense work and widening inequalities between occupations. At this stage, the announcement proves neither acquired skills nor better working conditions. In insurance, the value would lie in connecting learning to real situations: understanding an answer, spotting an omission and knowing when to take over. An attendance certificate is not yet proof of proficiency.

Useful training shows up in what people can do better — and reject more confidently — afterwards. Hours attended are not enough to measure the autonomy gained.

6Sovereignty & Infrastructure

2G shuts down. Old dependencies wake up.

Tomorrow's infrastructure sometimes begins with a forgotten device. Orange's timetable sets the 2G shutdown in Nice for 22 September, a milestone covered that day in Arcep's press review. The operator then plans two waves on 6 and 20 October, with an exception extending into December for designated coverage-gap areas. This announced timetable is not an audit of every device. And it goes beyond phones: Arcep's guidance, updated on 10 September, lists alarms, personal telecare, connected heating and some medical devices. Our take for prevention and assistance: a charged battery and a green light do not prove an alert will still arrive. Migration to a newer network needs checking end to end, right through to the signal's recipient. The quietest failure is a device that appears to work.

Check the network used, the maintenance agreement and whether an alert actually arrives: three questions more useful than how old the device looks.
