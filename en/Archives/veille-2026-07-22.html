Eleven days to the AI Act, cracking clay and an overflowing deficit

A new CatNat order and a home premium that inflates by 13% – Mistral puts wheels on its AI and signs with Seoul – Bercy discovers that 126 billion are missing – and there are 11 days left before the AI Act releases its fine book. Wednesday morning roundup. Here we go.

In 30 seconds

  • Insurance: new CatNat decree on 17 July, home premium +13 % (274→310 €), premium CatNat frozen 20%, clay under 55 % of the territory.
  • Tech & AI: Mistral launches Robostral Navigate and joins Naver Cloud; ARR's €930 million goal; Station F reopens its F/ai cohort in September.
  • Economy: growth 0.7%, inflation fell to 1.8% in June, but a deficit of 5% and a note of €126 billion to be paid by 2032.
  • Regulatory: J−11 before the AI Act, €35 million or 7% of the CA on the scale — while Accor and SFR are tested the cyber door in 48 hours.
1Insurance & Insurtech

The house cracks, the premium solidifies

The most costly disaster in France still makes no noise — just one new decree on the recognition of natural disasters published on 17 July, and walls that continue to split. The withdrawal-swelling of clays, which now covers 55 per cent of the territory and is the first compensation post of the CatNat scheme, pushes the climate bill towards EUR 143 billion accumulated between 2020 and 2050, i.e. +93 % compared to the previous generation. The client already cashes: the home premium leap of 13% in 2026, from 274 to 310 € per year, the vote which raised the CatNat premium to 20%, compared to 12% previously. In the incumbents, the mechanics rotate: AXA posted +6% premium in the first quarter at €38 billion — while his insured persons complain of increases of 15 to 20 % without any loss. And the insurtech, she, reloads: €1.3 billion raised in Europe in the first half of the year alone, on a French ecosystem of 150 actors and 3 billion euros accumulated.

The bonus can always go up, the hazard goes up faster. You no longer sell a blanket, you sell a chase between your fare and the climate — and the climate does not have a regulatory ceiling.

2Tech & AI

Mistral descends from the cloud, puts wheels and speaks Korean

The French champion understood that the next field is not written, he moves. Mistral has published Robostral Navigate, a robotic navigation model trained in simulation and pilotable with a single camera, while formalizing 8 July a global partnership with South Korean giant Naver Cloud and in preparing an open-weight Mixture-of-Experts model "fat but parsimonious" in early access. The financial fuel follows: from €18 million of recurrent revenues a year ago, the unicorn has reached €370 million from ARR and targets more than €930 million this year, backed by €4 billion of data centers. Around, the market confirms that the value has slipped from model to piping and agents: Together AI raises $800 million to $8.3 billion by leasing Nvidia clusters, We Research negotiates a $1.5 billion round, and a startup totally let his agent AI pilot his $100 million raise. Ecosystem side, Station F reopens its F/ai cohort in September with six new partners.

When an LLM lab sells robotic navigation and signs with Seoul, it is not dispersion, it is the admission that the margin is downstream of the model. Follow the money, not the demo.

3Economy & Finance

Growth in dungeon, short-term deficit

The French economic situation holds as an institute: "Reinvigorated Inflation, Fragile Growth". The Government has reduced growth 2026 to 0.7%, after falling GDP by 0.1% in the first quarter and a rebound expected of only +0.3% in the second — the good news of the month being a inflation fell to 1.8% in June due to energy reflux. But on the public finance side, four economists submitted Bercy on 15 July a report providing for, without correction, a deficit of 6.8% of GDP and a debt of 130.5% in 2030, with an addition of EUR 126 billion in efforts to be found by the end of the quinquennium. Bercy's response is holding for now in €3 billion in additional savings and growth in departmental spending limited to 0.4%either four times less than inflation expected in 2027. Statistical appointment: Second quarter GDP falls on July 30, provisional CPI on July 31.

A growth of 0.7% with a state that has to cut €126 billion is an oxygen-free market with a 2027 tax risk. Your price increases are trading in an economy that is no longer growing and a budget that is looking for revenue.

4Regulation & Risk

J−11: the AI Act releases its stem book

Eleven days. On 2 August, the AI Act activates national governance, supervisory powers and sanctions regime, with a calibrated scale to wake up comexes : €35 million or 7% of the global CA for prohibited practices, €15 million or 3% for a high-risk non-compliant system. In concrete terms, from that date onwards, any chatbot or content generator will have to report that we are talking to an AI and mark its synthetic content (Article 50) — legal grade which does not exempt from anything, Digital Omnibus repelling certain high-risk bonds but leaving August 2026 binding. And the AI Act never happens alone: it piles up with NIS2, DORA and the Cyber Resilience Act on the same fiscal year 2026. The risk is not waiting for the schedule: one leak attributed to Accor was claimed on 16 July, another attributed to SFR on 17 July., in a year more than 90 million accounts have been affected and the leak Cegedim has exposed 15 million health records.

In J−11, the question is no longer "we are concerned" but "who signs the inventory". An incomplete AI register is valid, and the regulator reads faster than your DSI does.

5People & Work

One in two uses AI, one in three has been trained

Adoption has exploded, jurisdiction is lagging behind — and the gap will be paid. According to APEC, one in two frames now uses AI at least once a week, i.e. +15 points in one yearBut the fracture is generational: 62% under 35 versus 38% over 55, and only 29% have received dedicated training — seven out of ten executives improvise on a tool that touches your customer data. The recruiter, on the other hand, runs under automated applications: the share of executives applying with an AI tool has doubled in a year, which explains that 73% of recruitment managers now place critical thinking first, ahead of pure technical skills. The market, cautiously, restarts: APEC is on the table 305,800 executive recruitments in 2026, i.e. +4%, in a much less tense market.

When dual use and training stagnates at 29%, you have not deployed a technology, you have outsourced a risk to your teams. The note is called data leak or non-explicable decision.

6Sovereignty & Infra

France captures billions, cables run to the American

Digital sovereignty leaves the colloquium for reinforced concrete. France received €109 billion in foreign investment in data centers in 2025 and aims to network 250 regional sites, worn by a gold geography : Marseille has become a global gateway with about 20 submarine cables connecting Europe to Africa, the Middle East and Asia. The hiccup is in the balance of power: submarine cables have returned to being a disputed strategic leverbut Big Techs have increased from 10% to 71% of international capacity control in 10 years. And behind the story of the tricolor revival, the investigation recalls who really benefits from the data center boom in France — often to the same American hyperscalers. Meanwhile, the giants' calendar is moving forward: the first gigawatt of the OpenAI-Nvidia partnership unfolds in the second half of 2026.

Your continuity plan is based on an energy–cable–jurisdiction chain that you do not control on any of the three links. The real subject is not sovereignty, it is substitutability.
